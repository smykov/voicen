## Context

T-110 round 2 redid the grep for stall-sensitive verdicts in `crates/voicen-core/tests/**`. It closed the test-side shape (the test thread must act before a product timer) with `dictation_session::with_premise`. The grep also found a server-side shape it did not fix: tests where a loopback mock must answer before a finite product deadline. A host stall longer than the margin turns them red on correct code. None of them has been seen failing in CI. They are listed with their margins in `docs/decisions/core-tests.md` › Open; smallest first:

- about 300 ms: the `ms` scenarios of `api_pipeline::no_event_or_failure_carries_key_query_or_transcript` and `diag_pipeline::log_holds_no_key_query_or_transcript_on_any_path`, and `local_download`'s retry after a stall;
- 350 ms per gap: `local_download::trickle_longer_than_no_data_succeeds_and_stall_fails`;
- about 2 s: every helper with `connect: 2 s`;
- 3.5 s: `local_server::request_deadline_is_local_server_not_api_transcription`;
- about 5 s: the helpers' 5 s totals;
- 9.5 s: the `post_process_timeout` default-snapshot leg (accepted in T-110 review 1).

The fix touches 6+ test files and their bite designs (T-048 refused-port constraints, download retries). The investigator decides per case: widen the margin where the deadline is a test parameter, check the premise as `with_premise` does, or propose a minimum in-time margin for the owner to accept as a T1 residual.

## Investigation

**Frame.** Observed: nothing yet. None of these tests has failed in CI. Expected: a correct deadline is never red because of a host stall (F-021: a stall of about 1.4 s on windows-latest, once in about 57 runs). The shape: a leg expects the counterparty to act before a finite product deadline. The counterparty is a loopback mock or `common::Server` thread, or the test thread. A host stall longer than the margin flips the outcome on correct code. This follows from reading the code. It is not a reproduction.

**How I looked.** Serena: `find_symbol` with bodies for `dictation_session::with_premise`, `voicen_core::timeouts::Timeouts` and `engine::http::client_with_read_timeout`, and `get_symbols_overview` of `local_models/download.rs` (`Downloader::new` is the only place that takes `Timeouts`; there is no setter and no per-start value). grep over `crates/voicen-core/tests/**`: `Timeouts {`, `fn timeouts`, `fn quick`, `set_delay`, `Stall`, `Trickle`, `SilentHeaders`, `END_WAIT`, `set_read_timeout`. grep over `crates/voicen-core/src` for `MockServer` / `TcpListener`: only `test_support/local_models.rs`, the `Server` itself, so no unit test under `src` has a loopback leg. The grep is not exhaustive: a deadline built at run time or passed through more layers would not show. context7 `/websites/rs_reqwest` (docs.rs latest; the lock pins 0.13.5, so this is not pinned): blocking `ClientBuilder::timeout` defaults to 30 s and applies to connect, read and write. The trickle test is what shows it is per read.

**Hypotheses.**
1. *The deadline is a test parameter and is short only because one `Timeouts` serves both the timeout leg and the in-time legs.* Confirmed for the redaction tests (`ms` shared by six immediate scenarios and `timeout`), the post-processing log test (`t` vs `t_pp_short`), `local_server` (5 s, while the bite is the 300 ms api value), the helpers (connect 2 s and 5 s totals, which no timeout leg relies on: every timeout leg found overrides its own field with 300 or 500 ms, or uses `form_timeouts`), and the `post_process_timeout` default leg (a control: "so the server does answer"). Fix: widen.
2. *The deadline is the subject and its bite caps the margin.* Confirmed for `local_download`'s stall, silent-headers and trickle legs. The bite "no read timeout configured" needs the end to come after `END_WAIT` and before HOLD, and the reqwest 30 s default sits in between, so `END_WAIT < 30 s`. The failure leg lasts at least `no_data`, and the trickle lasts several times `no_data`. The retry shares the `Downloader` and its `no_data`, because the slot release and `.part` cleanup are checked on the same instance. So no setting reaches BUDGET scale: margin = test cost. This needs an owner-accepted minimum M.
3. *The same concern on a second path: the test-thread shape that T-110 closed.* Confirmed, outside `dictation_session`. `cancel_during_stall_*` must cancel before `no_data` (800 ms) runs out after the first Progress. Its own bite note says a read timeout without the flag ends as `Failed{DownloadInterrupted}`, which is red. `cancel_of_another_model_*` (300 ms drain) and `second_start_while_one_runs_*` (200 ms sleep, two `start`s, accept counts) must cancel before `no_data` 2 s and the server's 3 s hold. T-110's grep classed `local_download`'s sleeps as "before absence checks" and missed these acts. The premise approach (`with_premise`) fits only where the stall is the subject (`cancel_during_stall`). In the other two the stall is incidental, and a long Trickle removes the race.
4. *This class was fixed before (`os-answer-deadline-race`: F-005, F-013, F-019, F-021; rca T-080, T-110).* Confirmed as background. Each fix drew the rule for the kinds it had found: refusal budget, resolver budget, wall-clock ceilings, the test-thread premise. T-110's Open names this server-side kind. Hypothesis 3 shows that T-110's grep, its T1 enforcement, was again incomplete.
5. *Hang-ender waits are themselves too short.* Partly. `END_WAIT` is 10 s in `common/download.rs`, `local_models_service.rs` and `local_model_delete.rs`, below BUDGET scale ("far above the time the awaited outcome needs", decisions/core-tests.md). In `common/download.rs` it is load-bearing (hypothesis 2), so it goes to about 20 s. The other two go to BUDGET. `openai_client::partial_body_server`'s 5 s server read timeout is a test-owned deadline of the same kind.

**Root cause.** See `analysis.root_cause`. It explains every listed margin and the three cancel tests. Contradicting evidence: none. No CI failure supports it either, so this is a matching shape, not a proven flake, as T-110 said.

**Options.**
- **A (recommended): one seam in `tests/common` plus per-case routing** (`analysis.approach`). It removes the cause: no in-time leg carries a short deadline unless it is listed at M, and the test thread never races a product timer. Red first: each changed test gets an injected stall past its old margin, red today and green after: a `set_delay` / `Serve` delay just past the old margin on the in-time leg, and a test-thread sleep before `cancel`. Bite check per test, recorded. Risk: run time grows by about 30-40 s of wall time across the stall tests at M = 5 s; it runs in parallel. Rollback: revert the commit.
- **B: premise checks for everything** (re-run when the in-time leg ended `Timeout` / `DownloadInterrupted`). Rejected. A product bug that always times out would then skip instead of going red. On the server side the outcome cannot tell a host stall from a too-short deadline, and that is exactly the bite.
- **C: accept every margin as a T1 residual.** Rejected for anything under M: 300 ms to 2 s margins are at or near F-021's measured 1.4 s.

**Owner decision points.**
- **D1, M (the minimum in-time margin for bite-bound deadlines, T1 residual).** Recommended: 5 s, about 3.5 × the 1.4 s stall F-021 measured. Cost: about 5-17 s per affected `local_download` test. The ceiling is about 25 s, because the margin must stay below the 30 s reqwest default with room for the bite. Alternatives: 2 s (cheaper, under 2 × the observed stall) or 10 s (each test about 10-30 s). Record it in `docs/decisions.md` and core-tests.md I2.
- **D2, mechanical enforcement (exit (c)-like, inside this task or a follow-up).** Recommended: yes, as a follow-up task with `checker: true`, so this task's diff stays out of `scripts/ci`. The check would be a token tripwire refusing `Timeouts {` and `Timeouts::default()` in voicen-core test files outside `tests/common`. Every deadline then comes from `in_time()`, from a named short constructor for timeout legs, or from the listed M cases, which turns the T1 classification into a T3 check. Alternative: keep T1 review, and accept that the grep has been incomplete twice.
