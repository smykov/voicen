## The four points the orchestrator asked about

**1. Do the new tests honestly kill 02, 15 and 16? Yes.** I settled this by reading and ran no mutation of my own (ordering is not a critical guarantee under decision #32).
- How the mock works: `ByChunkLength` reads the `data` size at RIFF+40 of the multipart body, the standard 44-byte header that `wav::encode` writes. It picks the entry within 2 s of that length. The lengths are 62, 70 and 78 s, so they are 8 s apart. The mock answers by content, never by arrival order, so the concurrent sending of chunks cannot make it flaky.
- Fixture design: each utterance is over 60 s and is followed by 1 s of silence. So no two utterances share a 120 s timed chunk, and the request count (`== 3`, or `== 4` with the floor group) pins the "one chunk per utterance" design. If the chunker ever cut differently, the test would fail loudly with `unknownmark` or a wrong count. It would not pass silently.
- 02 (`lines.reverse()`): the three mic lines give "echo foxtrot charlie delta alpha bravo" against the ordered reference, so `item1 s=0 d=0 i=0 n=6 wer=0.00%` is absent and the test turns red. This kill is an assertion failure, not a build error.
- 15 (audio spliced at the front): the item's audio becomes 78, 70, 62. The mock still answers each chunk by its length, so the hypothesis by `start_ms` is reversed against the manifest-order reference, and the group line changes.
- 16 (reference spliced at the front): the reference becomes "echo foxtrot charlie delta alpha bravo" against the ordered hypothesis, and the group line changes.
- The files are written in reverse manifest order, so a directory-order read would also fail.
- `.teamwright/logs/mutants/T-163-20261114T185407Z.txt` reports `3 killed, 0 survived`. The fixture words are fake, and `assert_no_secrets` covers them.
