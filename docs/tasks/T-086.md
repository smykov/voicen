## Context

Decision #120 adopted P-020 (PRINCIPLES 1.3.0): a guard decides on a platform switch, an owned literal or a raw token, never on its own model of another tool's grammar. T-085 applies it to `scripts/ci/release-guard.sh`. Its investigation found other guards built the same way, as awk readers of `ci.yml`: `scripts/ci/ci-wip.sh`, `scripts/ci/setup-node-cache.sh` and `scripts/ci/telegram-guard.sh`, plus the `shell-test-layout` lexer. None has a recorded leak, and none was probed.

T-060 also lists two related guards:
- `scripts/ci/http-client-builder.sh` (T-079 r1#1) is a list of names it refuses, a different mechanism.
- `app-acl.sh` (T-049 r1#4) is not in the tree at HEAD, so that item may be stale.

## Acceptance

- [ ] Each guard in `scripts/ci/` and `make check` is listed in `docs/decisions/ci-toolchain.md` with its `decides_on` (switch / literal / token) and what it names. A guard that passes on its own reading of a grammar is either rewritten to one of the three or marked `T-NNN` with the reason it cannot be yet.
- [ ] Failure branch: for each rewritten guard, a fixture that a grammar reader would miss (a folded or multi-line scalar, a variable command name, a comment-hidden key) is refused. Each fixture is red against the old guard.
- [ ] `make check` is green, and no guard keeps a tokenizer, scalar reader or comment reader.
